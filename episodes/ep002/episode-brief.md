# Episode brief — ep002 v1
Title (working): What Really Happens When You Ask AI a Question?
Audience: business professionals and curious beginners in Africa and the diaspora, no coding background
Learning objective (one, observable): after watching, the viewer can explain the four things that happen between typing a question and getting an answer (the words become tokens, the model predicts the next words from patterns, it only knows what is in its context, it cannot check anything unless it is given a tool), and can say why an answer can sound confident and still be wrong.
Supporting ideas (max 3): 1) the model has no memory of you or your farm unless it is told; 2) missing context is the most common cause of a bad answer, before "hallucination"; 3) a good answer for a real user needs context + a source + an escalation path to a human expert
Duration: 3.5 min main (build-story format, about 450 spoken words) · cutdown: 45 s vertical (scenes: hook, decide, constraint-fix, ownership or closing, chosen at storyboard)
Platforms: YouTube 16:9, Shorts/Reels/TikTok 9:16
Characters: tanyi, amara, kito, hallucinator (delivers the confident wrong answer), gatekeeper (escalation and accountability)
Central teaching point: an AI answer is a prediction made from patterns plus whatever context you gave it; give it the right context, a checked source and a human to escalate to, and it becomes useful
Proposed African setting (country, place, community, languages): Cameroon, a smallholder cocoa farmers' cooperative; region and languages to be verified by research (candidates: Centre region, French with Ewondo; or South-West region, English with Cameroon Pidgin). To be confirmed by the Researcher and the Story Director.
User need: a farmer named in the story brief notices brown pods and asks a general chatbot on a shared phone what to do; the answer is confident, generic and possibly harmful; the cooperative's field agent is one person for hundreds of farmers
Artifact the crew builds: a small question-answering assistant for the cooperative that adds context (crop, location, season, what the farmer sees), answers only from the cooperative's checked guidance, and hands unknown or risky cases to the field agent
Story status: fictional teaching scenario based on researched conditions
Story bank concept used: Farmer advisory (multilingual crop-support assistant with expert escalation), scoped down to the question-and-answer step so the episode stays about what happens when you ask
Safety angle: harmful advice risk (pesticides, dosage), confident wrong answers, human escalation, who is accountable for advice given to farmers
Ownership angle: the cooperative owns its guidance data and the assistant; the field agent maintains the guidance; value stays with members
Call to action (build challenge): take one question you ask AI at work, write down the context it would need to answer it well, and add "and tell me where this comes from"; compare the two answers
Must-use facts / stories: none. Do not invent statistics about cocoa, phones or connectivity; the Researcher sources every setting claim or it stays out
Constraints: build-story format, no invented statistics, stock voices only, fixed cast (the farmer and the field agent appear through chat UI and amara), disclosure line on the title scene
Open questions:
1. Region and language choice for the Cameroon setting (see candidates above). Default if no preference: let research decide which is better supported.
2. Should the cutdown target the "confident wrong answer" moment (most shareable) or the four-step explanation? Default: the wrong answer plus the fix.
3. Keep the working title, or use the story in the title (for example "Why the Chatbot Gave the Farmer a Dangerous Answer")? Default: working title now, three options at publishing.
Decisions at gate 1 (Hippolyte, approved): setting to verify first = South-West region, English / Cameroon Pidgin, with the security context checked; cutdown = the wrong answer plus the fix; keep the working title.
Status: approved v1
