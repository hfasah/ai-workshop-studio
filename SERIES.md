# AI With Hippolyte — Series Bible

**Tagline:** Complex AI. Explained visually. Built practically.

**World:** The AI Workshop, a virtual laboratory where business problems arrive and are turned into working AI systems.

**Host character:** Tanyi, an animated stand-in for the real host (the series is still called "AI With Hippolyte"): a middle-aged Black African AI engineer who has built and operated production systems. Direct, warm, practical, no hype. Never a cartoon for children; this is professional education with characters as memory aids.

**Positioning (never a generic "AI tips" channel):**
- How AI systems actually work
- How to build AI employees and agents
- Reliability, hallucination control, evaluation
- AI infrastructure and production deployment
- Practical AI for businesses and job seekers
- Responsible AI implementation
- African and global examples missing from mainstream AI education

**Learning model.** Every episode moves the viewer through **Understand AI → Recognize Africa's opportunity → Take action**, guided by the BUILD framework from *Africa's AI Moment*: **B**uild African capability, **U**se AI to solve African problems, **I**nvest in African innovation, **L**ead responsibly, **D**evelop African ownership. The central story: African characters do not simply discuss AI. They use local knowledge to build real systems that Africans can use, maintain and own.

**Short format (default product, 45–60 seconds).** One concept, one story moment, one action. This is what gets published daily; long episodes are the exception.

| # | Beat | Time | What happens | Scene types | Cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 0–4 s | The claim or the answer in the first sentence, with a named African user and the consequence. No greeting, no title, no "in this video". | statement | tanyi |
| 2 | Turn | 4–18 s | The wrong assumption or the failure moment (hallucinator or a real constraint), shown, not told. | demo (chat) or dialogue | kito, hallucinator or amara |
| 3 | Mechanism | 18–35 s | The one idea that explains it, as a flow or a compare with three nodes at most. | flow or compare | tanyi |
| 4 | Fix in action | 35–48 s | The African characters make it work: one demo step with a source, a permission or a human hand-off. | demo | kito, gatekeeper |
| 5 | Action and closing | 48–60 s | One thing to try, then "Build it to work reliably." (short closing) or the full signature line if time allows. | statement or outro | tanyi |

Visual rule: every short carries at least one drawn diagram (a `diagram` or `flow` scene with real icons and connectors) and one real-world examples tile set (`examples` scene: three jobs or businesses where the idea applies), because people remember pictures and uses, not definitions.

Short-format rules: 110–150 spoken words, 5 scenes (never a title scene; the brand chip and disclosure appear in the description and the first card), no line over 20 words, a visual change every 5–8 seconds, captions on every word, the first frame already shows the claim. Hook patterns that work: a surprising consequence ("It sent 500 emails. Twelve were wrong."), a direct contradiction ("Your chatbot is not looking anything up."), a question the viewer has asked themselves, a number that reframes. Never clickbait that the episode does not pay off.

**Build-story format (long form, 3–4 minutes, for courses and the occasional YouTube long episode).** The nine-step story engine mapped onto timed beats and scene types:

| # | Beat | Time | What happens | Scene types | Cast |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook | 10 s | A named person meets a concrete problem in an existing workflow. | statement | tanyi |
| 2 | Title | 5 s | Series, episode, title, disclosure line (fictional or verified). | title | none |
| 3 | Listening | 25 s | The crew hears the user and a local domain specialist (their words in chat or list UI, relayed by amara). | dialogue, demo (chat) | amara, tanyi |
| 4 | Decide | 30 s | What AI should and should not do here. Understand AI. | compare, flow | tanyi, gatekeeper |
| 5 | Build | 45 s | kito builds a small working version, step by step. | demo | kito, amara |
| 6 | Constraint and fix | 30 s | A realistic failure or constraint appears (hallucinator or a local reality); they improve data, interface, architecture or guardrail. | demo, dialogue | kito, hallucinator, tanyi |
| 7 | User test | 20 s | The user tries the improved system; what works, what still needs a human. | demo (chat, approval) | kito, gatekeeper |
| 8 | Ownership | 20 s | Who maintains it, who owns the data and IP, who receives the value, who is accountable. | bullets | gatekeeper, tanyi |
| 9 | Build challenge and closing | 20 s | One practical action or build challenge, then the signature closing. | statement, outro | tanyi |

**Concept format (2–4 minutes)**, the older long format used by the pilot:
1. Hook (10s): a striking, concrete consequence. Example: "An AI agent can send 500 emails while you sleep. But what if it sends the wrong information?"
2. Problem (20s): a familiar workplace situation, usually Amara asking.
3. Visual explanation (60–90s): characters, diagrams, metaphors, short demonstrations.
4. Practical example (45–60s): the concept applied to recruiting, IT operations, customer service, or another business workflow. Kito often demonstrates; sometimes the Hallucinator interferes.
5. Safety lesson (20s): validation, permissions, privacy, human approval. The Gatekeeper's moment.
6. Action step (15s): one thing the viewer can try today.
7. Signature closing, spoken by Tanyi: "Don't just use AI. Build it to work reliably."

**Authenticity rules:**
- Africa is diverse countries, markets, languages, institutions, cities, rural areas and diaspora communities. Verify the actual setting; never transfer a fact from one country to another.
- African characters are builders, entrepreneurs, researchers, workers, investors, policymakers and leaders with different expertise and viewpoints.
- No saviour narratives, poverty spectacle, generic villages, token cultural symbols, invented customs, invented local-language phrases, invented organisations or invented deployment success.
- AI is a multiplier, not a substitute for schools, hospitals, broadband, electricity, institutions, capital or effective government. Africa is not "catching up with" or copying Silicon Valley.
- Local users and specialists are co-designers. Show who maintains the system, owns the data, receives the economic value and is accountable when it fails.
- Declare fictional teaching scenarios as fictional; declare verified case studies with their source.

**Voice and style rules:**
- Plain English. One idea per sentence. Analogies before jargon; define jargon once when it appears.
- Show systems thinking: inputs, steps, tools, checks, outputs.
- Every factual claim must be something the host could defend. No invented statistics.
- Humour comes from the characters (Kito's believable mistakes, the Hallucinator's confidence), not from sarcasm at the viewer.

**Story bank:** twelve fictional starting points (farmer advisory, classroom tutor, rural clinic support, market business assistant, job opportunity navigator, government document service, power reliability monitor, flood warning service, local logistics planner, African-language service desk, cross-border trade helper, cultural archive assistant) in `team/STORY-BANK.md`. Each needs a researched country, community, language, regulatory setting and user workflow before production.
